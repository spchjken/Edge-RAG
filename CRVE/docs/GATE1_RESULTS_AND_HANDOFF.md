## What opportunity survives

These are corpus-macro point estimates. NearBestHit uses the frozen rho=0.90 and material gain
delta=0.005. Rates condition on the relevant opportunity existing; they are not success rates over
all 200 queries. See [the metric implementation](../src/crve/selection/gate1_metrics.py).

| Policy | Nominal cap | Mean candidates | NearBestHit | ReferenceBOR | RecallHit@1000 | SafeDocOppRecall@1000 | Material TermRecall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Extended RRF | 200 | 200.0 | 56.2% | 74.2% | 91.1% | 73.9% | 16.9% |
| Lexical2 RRF | 200 | 190.8 | 60.6% | 73.7% | 88.8% | 68.5% | 21.1% |
| Extended RRF | 400 | 400.0 | 66.8% | 81.7% | 94.4% | 81.3% | 26.6% |
| Lexical2 RRF | 400 | 376.8 | 65.6% | 80.0% | 95.1% | 77.3% | 31.8% |
| Union of PPMI200 and Sparse200 | 400 | 331.1 | 64.0% | 77.7% | 95.1% | 76.4% | 27.8% |
| Extended RRF | 500 | 500.0 | 71.9% | 83.7% | 95.4% | 83.7% | 29.6% |
| Lexical2 RRF | 500 | 468.4 | 72.3% | 83.9% | 95.1% | 79.3% | 36.4% |

Rounded values follow the budget CSV. For the milestone boundary, Lexical2@400 BOR is 79.98%,
slightly below 80%; Extended@400 BOR is 81.69% and its NearBestHit is 66.85%.

Extended combines WholeQueryBGE, AnchorBGEFiltered, PPMISidecar, SparseLexicalContextProfiles and
AcronymDefinitionRescue. Lexical2 fuses only PPMISidecar and SparseLexicalContextProfiles. Both use
RRF k=60 over input ranks through 500. Union400 retains the deduplicated top 200 from each lexical
channel without refilling to 400.

Extended@400 is a reasonable balanced reference for Gate 2 research: about two thirds of materially
addressable queries retain a near-best term under corpus-macro averaging, while 81.69% of reference
best-gain opportunity and 81.27% of safe document opportunity survive. It captures 18.9 materially
ranking-helpful terms per addressable query under corpus-macro averaging. Lexical2@400 and Union400
capture 22.4 and 20.0 on that same averaging convention. These counts support testing whether Gate 2
can recognize useful terms among many candidates.

The matched-count comparison also keeps the lexical alternative credible. With both sets matched
to Union400's candidate count on each query (mean 331.1), Union400 versus Extended gives NearBestHit
64.00% versus 61.46%, BOR 77.72% versus 77.73%, and safe document opportunity 76.37% versus 76.04%.
Their paired descriptive intervals span zero for these three differences. Union400's RecallHit
advantage is 4.29 percentage points, with a descriptive 95% interval of [0.59, 10.42]. Matching output
counts does not equalize proposer computation or the number of unique contexts Gate 2 must score.

Interpretation limits relevant to the next stage:

- The reference universe contains material ranking opportunity for 134/200 queries and safe recall
  opportunity for 121/200. Per-corpus rates differ substantially; SciFact has only one recall-addressable
  query. Continue to report per-corpus denominators alongside macro coverage.
- TermRecall measures the fraction of reference helpful terms retained. TermPrecision measures the
  helpful fraction of selected terms. A low TermRecall does not imply that the surviving set contains
  too few useful options, and a low TermPrecision does not classify all other candidates as harmful.
- A helpful term has at least one useful, safe tested weight. Gate 2 still has to select it and Gate 3
  still has to choose its influence. BOR and document opportunity are oracle retention measures, not
  achieved nDCG or Recall improvements from a deployed expansion.
- For the same single-term action space, a pure rejection gate cannot recover an opportunity removed
  by Gate 1. Multi-term retrieval effects require their own evaluation; individual gains do not add.

## Research decision and next work

Close the current Gate 1 proposer comparison without claiming a frozen-protocol pass. There is enough
retained opportunity to justify Gate 2 research; whether it is sufficient for an effective final
retrieval system remains an empirical question. Do not extend the proposer search or raise the
candidate cap solely to cross a development threshold.

The next study should:

1. Use existing candidate ranks and action labels as development evidence. Keep Extended RRF as a
   balanced reference and retain the lexical policies as alternatives; final policy selection remains
   open. Carry a small, declared comparison into Gate 2 rather than reopening the complete proposer grid.
2. Measure Gate 2 precision, fixed-action harm rejection and lost opportunity relative to each incoming
   candidate set. Report both additional retention from C1 to C2 and total retention relative to the
   reference universe, so filtering cannot hide useful candidates it removes.
3. Use L=200 as the frozen control and L=400 as a candidate research condition while measuring actual
   latency, host RAM, VRAM, unique context count and term-context references. The L=500 curve remains
   useful diagnostic evidence, not an automatic budget choice because it crosses a milestone.
4. Preserve abstention and reserve held-out queries or corpora for later selection/weighting and final
   retrieval claims. Reuse the current 200 queries as development data, without treating them as a new
   independent test after policy selection.

No Gate 1 rerun is required to close this study or start Gate 2 research. The frozen L=200 contract
remains intact; any larger operational cap needs a separately declared decision supported by downstream
cost and quality evidence. The original 85% Material TermRecall floor remains recorded historically,
but is not a prerequisite for this research transition.
